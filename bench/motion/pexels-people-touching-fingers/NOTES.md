# pexels-people-touching-fingers / seg01: notes

Files:
- `clip.json`: the clip. It is generated by `build.py` (run `python build.py`), so edit the anchors there, not the JSON.
- `out.mp4`: fframes render, 1080x1920, 30 fps, 8.9 s.
- `strip.png`: player strip.
- `work/sbs.mp4`: the reference and my render side by side.
- `work/`: measurement and compare scripts (`track.py`, `track2.py` template-track the reference; `evaltips.py` compares fingertip positions; `cmp.sh`/`cmp.py` overlay frames; `speed.py` gives the per-frame fingertip speed).

Render command, run from this folder:
```
fframes-clip.exe --clip clip.json --assets ".../assets" --background "#120605" --spotlight 0.2745,0.50,0.235,#dcaaa6 render -o out.mp4
```

## What the reference does

Positions are fractions of the frame (x of the width, y of the height). They are the index fingertips, measured by eye on gridded frames and, from 0.3 s on, by OpenCV template tracking.

- **0.0 s.** The bottom-left arm is already moving fast. Its hand is low (index tip about 0.52, 0.83) and points right, nearly level. The index is extended and the other fingers droop, loosely curled. The top-right hand is only a sliver at the right edge.
- **0.0-0.5 s.** Both arms sweep in fast, and each motion is an ease-out that is already at full speed on frame 0.
  - Bottom hand: rises almost straight up (tip y 0.83, 0.72, 0.65, 0.615) and rotates from about 10 deg to about 25 deg upward.
  - Top hand: comes in from the right edge, hanging with its fingers pointing straight down (tip x 1.0, 0.93, 0.85, 0.75, 0.70). Its fingers swing toward down-left.
- **0.5-1.0 s.** Both hands slow down. The bottom index tilts to about 45 deg and its middle, ring and pinky open a little. The top hand turns to point down-left at about 47 deg. The tips close to about 0.55, 0.455 (top) and 0.47, 0.51 (bottom).
- **1.0-1.9 s.** A slow final creep: the top tip goes 0.55 to 0.50 and the bottom tip 0.51 to 0.465. The bottom hand settles by about 1.7 s and the top by about 1.85 s.
  - Final pose: the index tips are about 30 px apart horizontally and 15 px vertically (top 0.50, 0.454; bottom 0.47, 0.466), in the Creation of Adam pose.
  - Top hand (I read it as a right hand): palm faces down-right, thumb toward the camera and hanging down with its nail visible. Index straight, middle and ring curled under it.
  - Bottom hand (a left hand): palm faces down-right, index straight up-right, middle pointing right, ring and pinky curled, thumb lying along the middle finger.
- **1.9-8.5 s.** The hold. Only tiny drifts, all within about 1.5% of the width (15 px):
  - The top hand creeps left about 0.012 by 7 s, then rises about 0.006 from 8.0 to 8.5 s.
  - The bottom hand wobbles about 0.005 in x (to the right around 2.5 and 5 s, left around 6 s).
- **8.55-8.9 s.** Both hands start to pull apart and the clip ends mid-move:
  - Top: right and slightly down, +0.045 x by 8.88 s.
  - Bottom: down and right, +0.035 y and +0.017 x.

## How I matched it

- **Framing.**
  - Camera at (0, 0, 1.04) looking at the origin with fov 70. This is close to a phone's portrait field of view, and the strong perspective lets the short 0.26 m rig forearms reach out of frame (see the problems below).
  - Spotlight: from a pixel profile of the reference, the centre is at 0.488 W, 0.50 H, so x = 0.2745 in height units. The gradient radius 0.235 gives the reference's half-brightness radius of about 0.2 H. Colour #dcaaa6 on background #120605.
- **Hands and orientation.**
  - Top hand: right, `place` palm toward down-right and slightly toward the camera.
  - Bottom hand: left, palm toward down-right and slightly away from the camera, so the thumb is on the camera side.
  - I reached this after one wrong first guess (palms to the camera), which gave a fanned "claw" look.
  - Small negative `mcp_abd` keeps the curled fingers together, as in the reference.
- **Placement by fingertip.**
  - `build.py` describes each hand by anchors: index-tip screen position, tip depth, the image angle of the fingers, the z tilt of the fingers, `wrist_flex`, and finger values.
  - For every key it runs `handkit fk` on the hand at the origin and solves for the `place.wrist` that puts the index tip exactly at the target screen point. It also projects the elbow, to make sure the forearm's rounded end stays outside the frame.
- **Approach (0-1.95 s).**
  - 18 keys at 0.1-0.15 s spacing with `"ease": "linear"`, sampled from a PCHIP curve through 11 measured anchors per hand. The last key is `smooth`.
  - This reproduces the ease-out that is already moving on frame 0: the speed falls from about 32 px/frame to 0 with no stall.
  - Clip-wide `"follow": 0` (see the problems below).
- **Hold.** 10 keys with the default natural ease, each moving the tips by the tracked offsets, plus `"idle": {"amount": 0.6}`.
- **Pull-back.** One key at t = 9.2 with move 0.7, so it starts at 8.5 s and the clip ends mid-move, as in the reference.
- **Result.** Fingertip error against the tracked reference (`work/tips.txt`):
  - 0-8.5 s: within about 10 px on a 1080x1920 frame, mostly 1-6 px. One 16 px outlier at 0.27 s; the 27 px at 0.57 s is a bad tracker sample in the reference data.
  - Pull-back: about 10 px behind the reference at 8.78 s.

## Honest assessment

**Close:**
- Composition, the screen paths and timing of both index tips, and the deceleration profile.
- The two-phase approach: a fast sweep, then a slow creep that ends around 1.7-1.9 s.
- The near-touch gap, the tiny hold drifts, and the start of the pull-back at the end.
- The overall hand shapes read correctly: a straight pointing index on both hands, curled fingers under it, and the top thumb hanging with its nail toward the camera.

**Different:**
- **Arms.** The rig forearms are thin uniform capsules. The reference arms are broad and taper, and the top arm is much longer in frame. I had to angle the forearms steeply toward the camera to get their ends out of frame, so the arms look slightly too thin and their angle differs a little from the reference near the frame edges.
- **Depth.** The top hand sits about 22 cm nearer the camera than the bottom hand. Their fingertips are only almost touching in projection, not in 3D. This was needed to keep the top forearm's end out of frame, and it also matches the top hand looking about 15% bigger in the reference. It is invisible in the video.
- **Finger detail.**
  - The bottom thumb sits a bit lower; in the reference it lies along the middle finger.
  - The top middle and ring fingers are slightly more separated than in the reference.
  - The tiny finger movements during the hold are not matched individually (idle breathing stands in for them).
- **Not matched, and ignored per the brief:** lighting, the shadows the hands cast on the wall, the reference's darkness of the arms outside the spotlight, its vignette, and the dim halo around the spot (the player's gradient cannot make a halo).

## Problems, confusions and missing features in Handkit

1. **No ease for a clip that starts mid-motion, and no velocity continuity across keys.**
   - `natural`, `smooth` and `snappy` all start from zero speed. I sampled a one-key move with `"spring": {"hz": 1, "damping": 1}` and got values 0.005, 0.015, 0.036, 0.07, 0.119 at t = 0-0.4.
   - Consecutive keys never carry speed through. In a test, a `linear` key followed by a `smooth` key made the hand stall to zero speed at the boundary.
   - With overlapping moves, a later key blends from the in-progress pose (value = lerp(prev(t), target, ease)), but the speed still jumps when the earlier move ends.
   - I expected an `ease-out`, or a spline ("through") ease that passes through keys with continuous velocity. I had to hand-build the curve as 18 dense `linear` keys from my own PCHIP interpolation.
   - `check` reports "pops: none" for the linear-then-smooth stall, so velocity discontinuities are not detected.
2. **`spring` seems to override `ease`.** With the same `spring`, `ease: "snappy"` and `ease: "natural"` produced identical samples at every time I tested. I expected snappy to overshoot.
3. **`follow` lags at t = 0 when the first move is fast.** Fingertip speed came out 19, 33, 47, 53, 31 px/frame over the first five frames: the fingers lag, then whip, because the system assumes the hand was at rest before t = 0. `follow` is clip-wide only, so I set `"follow": 0` and lost all finger follow-through. A per-key or per-hand follow, or an "already moving at t = 0" flag, would fix this.
4. **No screen-space or fingertip placement.** The hard part of matching video is "this fingertip is at this pixel". `place` only takes the wrist. `aim` and `touch` goals work in world space and cannot pin a site to a point. I wrote an fk-based solver (`build.py`, `solve_state`).
   - A `place: { site: "right.index.tip", at: [x, y, z] }` would remove that whole layer.
   - So would a goal like `{ "at_screen": "right.index.tip", "xy": [0.5, 0.45] }`.
5. **The forearm is short (0.26 m) and ends in a visible rounded cap.**
   - At a natural framing (fov 40 at 2 m) both forearms ended inside the frame as floating capsules.
   - Fix: fov 70, forearms tilted 20-25 deg toward the camera, and the top hand moved 22 cm nearer.
   - I had to project the elbow myself to verify it. `check`'s framing test only looks at fingertips, so it does not catch the far worse "arm stump in frame" problem.
   - An option to extend the arm off-screen (an upper arm, or a long forearm) would help.
6. **Keys are global across both hands.** From the spec ("every key starts from the previous key"), a key that names only one hand should hold the other hand at its previous key's pose for that key's timing. That would stall a hand in the middle of a linear chain. I did not test this; I avoided it by writing both hands into every key, which is 29 keys x 2 hands of mostly redundant JSON. Per-hand tracks would make independent timing easier.
7. **`check` framing reports cannot be marked as intentional.** The entering hand makes `check` always end in "1 problem(s)".
8. **`fk` has no `--json`**, unlike `query`. I parsed its text output with a regex.
9. **`sample` rewrites `place` as `{elbow, forearm_rotation_deg}`.** This makes the sampled pose hard to compare with the keys I wrote. I worked around it by running `fk`.
10. **Frame counts disagree.** `check` says 268 frames for 8.9 s. The player renders frames 0..267 (267 frames), and the strip's last frame is 8.87 s.
11. **The player cannot match the spotlight shape.** Its spotlight is a fixed gradient (opaque to 0.72 r, then a linear fade) with no halo or vignette. Minor, since lighting is not judged.
12. **The player exe needs the FFmpeg DLLs even for `--help`.** Without them on PATH, `frame --help` printed nothing at all, and plain `--help` failed with a missing `swscale-10.dll`. This is documented in the task, but the silent case is confusing.
13. **No way to read hand identity and palm direction off a pose quickly.** The `fingers_toward x palm_facing` thumb rule is precise but slow to reason with. A `render --views hero` with an axis or "thumb side" overlay would have saved one round of wrong guesses. (This is mostly about reading the reference, not a Handkit bug.)

## Iterations

About 12 render-and-compare rounds:
- 3 static-pose calibration renders: scale, camera and fov, and forearm cut-off.
- About 6 full-clip renders: palm orientation, finger shapes, `mcp_abd`, `follow`, and the pull-back timing.
- 3 motion tests on toy clips: linear and smooth key behaviour, overlap blending, and spring with snappy or natural.

Each round was checked with overlays against the reference and with fingertip numbers against tracked data.
